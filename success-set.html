Success set: test cases for evaluation and mode analysis

Drafted v0.1 · eight papers supplied by the convenor, October 20, 2026 · coded from full text · metrics from OpenAlex · not yet discussed

  • Positive test cases. A scoring rule, a mode agent, or an evaluation protocol that cannot recognize these as advances, reproduce what they report, or catch what a careful reader would catch, fails.
  • Slides: modes-meeting deck.
  • Each paper is used three ways: metrics (section 1), a mode coding on graph v0.4 (section 2), and a historical-evaluation case (section 3).
  • Full codings, with a page for every claim: private/analysis/success-set/. Metric values: private/success-set-indicators.json. Reading copies: the convenor’s shared folder.

What the metrics show on the recognized four

  • Citation impact separates them from ordinary papers, as the selection rule guarantees. The rule therefore cannot test whether citations identify advances.
  • Interdisciplinary input and uptake disagree. Shapiro and colleagues imported statistical physics, ultrasonics, and helioseismology (section 2), and 29% of their references are outside geoscience, but only 8% of their citers are. Rodell and colleagues imported satellite geodesy and government statistics, and 24% of their citers are outside.
  • CD₅ never comes out positive for any paper with enough citers. Noise tomography, the clearest new method, scores lowest.

Against matched controls

  • Design. For each paper, three research articles from the same journal and year, in the same OpenAlex subfield, at the median citation rank of that pool. Type article, at least 20 references (5 for 1963, when reference lists were short), retrieved anonymously on October 20, 2026. The same measures as above.
  • The citation percentile cannot separate advances inside these journals. A median-ranked Earth-science article in Nature or Science already sits at the 98.4th to 99.9th field-normalized percentile. The 99th-percentile selection rule used for the recognized papers selects journals, not breakthroughs. Use raw citations or rank within the journal-year instead.

  • No Nature or Science paper fails by citations. Of the 1,717 Earth-science research articles in the two journals from 2005 to 2015, none sits below its field’s median normalized citation; the median is the 99.4th percentile, and 34 (2%) are below the 90th. Stratum D is low only against its journal: 73 to 118 citations is the cut-off.
  • Failure, as now defined, is not visible in these strata. Failure means no conclusion reached; a contradicted claim is a successful result (convenor, October 21, 2026). Every published paper in C and D reports a conclusion, so neither stratum contains a failure. Later contradictions of Davis et al. (2006) and Wookey and Helffrich (2008) would make them successes of the field, not failures of the papers. A failure corpus has to come from work that ended without a conclusion: conference abstracts never followed by a paper, funded projects without publications, registered reports with inconclusive results.
  • Highly cited is not broad. The median share of citers outside geoscience is 4% for stratum C, the same as the matched controls with 30 or more citers (4%), and 7% for stratum D. The recognized four reach about 12%, carried by Lam and Rodell. Breadth of uptake does not follow citation success.
  • The citation tail is products. Among the most-cited Earth-science items, reanalyses, calibration curves, and assessment reports come first (ERA5: 31,639 citations); in 2019 to 2023 the first research article ranks seventh. Seven of the ten C papers contribute a method or an instrument.
  • Caveats. 366 topics that appear among these papers’ citers were not in the audited map and default to outside, which raises the outside shares. Stratum D’s five-year counts are mostly below 30. None of the 20 has been coded by mode yet; 2 have legal open full texts in hand, 2 more are free on the publisher site, 16 are paywalled.

What the eight show about modes

  • None entered from a hypothesis. Four entered from a capability, three from a problem, one from an anomaly in a repeat survey.
  • Node I (estimate a latent quantity) appears in 7 of 8; node N (new capability) in 6; node E (explore) in 6. Node D, designing a discriminating observation before the data, appears once (Lam’s verification design).
  • Rivals were stated in six papers and excluded by a test in four, one of them only partly. Xu and colleagues list eight possible mechanisms and exclude none; the retained account combines several (p. 479).
  • Every claim gate was met at least in part; no action gate was. On these eight, the claim gate describes the practice and the action gate does not.
  • The protective move at P appears three times: Vine and Matthews’s auxiliaries (p. 949), Normandeau and colleagues’ multi-year ice to close a 0.05 m versus 0.2 m gap (p. 10), and Xu and colleagues’ added assumption after a failed length prediction (p. 479).
  • Interdisciplinary content does not predict interdisciplinary uptake: Xu and colleagues import fracture mechanics, laboratory rupture experiments, and satellite radar, and 4% of their citers are outside geoscience.
  • None of the recognized four uses the word “first” for its claim; three of the four designated papers do. With eight papers this is a description, not a pattern.

What this page does not establish

  • That these eight are advances by any measure other than the convenor’s judgment and, for four of them, citations.
  • That the metrics discriminate advances: three controls per paper is a start, not a test with power, and the recognized four were chosen on the measure that separates them best. The designated four are the test; their citations mean something only after a few years.
  • That the codings are reliable: one coder per paper, from the text alone. Two independent codings of a subset would give an agreement figure.
