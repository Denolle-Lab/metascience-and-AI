Is there a scientific method?

Meeting 1 | Tuesday, September 8, 2026 | 1:00-2:00 PM Pacific

Central question

Is hypothesis-testing a universal account of inquiry, a standard for justification, or one useful mode among several?

The paper pair

Every citation on this page links to its full record in the bibliography, which also lists this meeting’s readings with access notes.

Pairing type: Methodological ideal / evidential counterpoint.

Platt, J. R. (1964). Strong Inference. Science, 146(3642), 347–353. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.146.3642.347

Read Platt in full. Identify the role of alternative explanations and discriminating observations.

Cleland, C. E. (2001). Historical science, experimental science, and the scientific method. Geology, 29(11), 987–990. https://doi.org/10.1130/0091-7613(2001)029%3C0987:HSESAT%3E2.0.CO;2

Read Cleland’s Geology paper in full. It makes the argument in four pages, for geologists; the discussant brings the rest.

Why these papers belong together

Platt offers a prescriptive strategy; Cleland analyzes differences between experimental interventions and reconstructing past events. Neither paper directly records this group’s daily research. Test both against an actual project rather than declaring one a complete scientific method. (Platt 1964; Cleland 2001)

Discussant-led paper

One rotating discussant takes the following in depth and brings its argument into the discussion; everyone else reads the abstract.

Cleland, C. E. (2002). Methodological and Epistemic Differences between Historical Science and Experimental Science. Philosophy of Science, 69(3), 474–496. https://doi.org/10.1086/342455

The full version of the Geology paper above: the time asymmetry that makes trace-hunting a rational strategy rather than a consolation prize, the “smoking gun”, and the reply to the charge that historical science is epistemically inferior. The discussant brings what the four-page version leaves out. (Cleland 2002)

Prepare before the meeting

Read the glossary and bring one definition you would change, with the paper of your own that it fails on. Bring one important paper and a short reconstruction of how the work actually proceeded. Mark which steps are documented and which are inferred.

Discussion questions

  1. Can an observational study discriminate hypotheses without controlling the system?
  2. Which research actions are not well described as a test?
  3. Are we describing how ideas are generated or how claims are warranted?

One-hour meeting

Time Activity
0-10 min Independent first judgments; surface disagreements.
10-25 min Compare the papers: claim, evidence, assumptions, and limits. The discussant presents the discussant-led paper in this block.
25-45 min Work through the case exercise below.
45-55 min Translate the discussion into agent requirements and tests.
55-60 min Record an output and one unresolved disagreement.

Case exercise

Reconstruct a project as initial knowledge -> observation -> representation -> question -> proposed explanation -> check. Compare that trajectory with the published narrative. Do not invent a private chronology from the article structure.

Definitions to fix in this meeting

Five terms that the rest of the quarter measures need a first working definition today, so that meetings 10 to 12 and the corpus study define their variables before seeing the data rather than after: field-shaping, novelty, depth, interdisciplinarity, and impact. The glossary proposes one for each, with the standard measure and what it leaves out. Agree on wording, or record the disagreement and who holds which side. Do not settle whether the measures are valid; that is the work of meetings 10 to 12.

Agent-design or evaluation output

Inquiry-action vocabulary v1: the glossary as amended in this meeting. Its modes of inquiry are the rows of the ledger and the codebook for the corpus study; its terms of assessment are the variables the quarter measures. Note where an agent should abstain or seek more evidence.

Optional extensions

  • Chamberlin (1965): The Method of Multiple Working Hypotheses. Science (1965). Written by a geologist, and the source Platt built on. Chamberlin’s target is the single ruling hypothesis; his remedy is to hold a family of them at once. Decide whether Platt’s loop and Chamberlin’s family are the same practice.
  • Simon (1973): Does Scientific Discovery Have a Logic?. Philosophy of Science (1973). Simon argues, against Popper, that discovery is problem solving with describable heuristics. The computer discovery programs listed in meeting 13 were built on this argument.
  • Klahr and Dunbar (1988): Dual Space Search During Scientific Reasoning. Cognitive Science (1988). People solving a discovery task searched two spaces, one of hypotheses and one of experiments, and some made progress by running experiments with no hypothesis at all. Experimental evidence that inquiry has more than one search mode.
  • Holmes (1987): Scientific Writing and Scientific Discovery. Isis (1987). Holmes compared laboratory notebooks with the published papers written from them and describes what the paper leaves out. The basis for standing question 5.

Record after the meeting

Record the evidence for your main claim, what changed your mind, what remains unresolved, and one change to the agent or its evaluation. Add the episode’s row to the inquiry-mode ledger. Keep confidential examples in private group notes rather than committing them to this public book.
