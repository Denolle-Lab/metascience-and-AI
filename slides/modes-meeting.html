1 · The modes3 / 31
A mode of inquiry is a kind of epistemic work
  • Defined by the gain it aims at and the evidence it owes.
  • Closest precedent: four "modes of research practice": hypothesis-driven, exploratory, question-driven, technology-oriented (O'Malley, Elliott & Burian 2010).
  • Moving between modes is "methodological iteration" (Elliott 2012).
  • Why it matters here: each mode will become a specialist agent, scored against the evidence its mode owes.
O'Malley, Elliott & Burian (2010) Studies in Hist. Phil. Biol. Biomed. Sci.; Elliott (2012)

1 · The modes5 / 31
Three modes have no defining source in the literature
  • Methods and computation. Nearest: Hacking's "algorismic" style (Hacking 2002).
  • Inferential estimation. Its founding texts are geophysical: Backus & Gilbert 1968, Tarantola 2005.
  • Replication of a one-off natural event. Replication is defined for experiments; the nearest is "conceptual replication", a test with different methods (Schmidt 2009).
  • Contested boundaries: instruments vs methods; exploration vs regularities; synthesis joins two literatures (meta-analysis and reconstruction from traces).

2 · Cleland's schema7 / 31
Cleland's schema: H + A + C ⇒ E
  • H the hypothesis; A auxiliary assumptions (theory of the instrument, forward model, processing, background laws); C the conditions, set or found; E the expected observation.
  • A mismatch refutes the conjunction H + A + C, not H alone (Duhem). Every verdict must say which of A and C was varied, and which was revised.
  • Example (seasonal seismic velocity change): H = groundwater storage; A = depth sensitivity of the surface waves, clock and instrument stable; C = found, wells outside the array; E = velocity lags rainfall by weeks.
Cleland (2001) Geology 29:987; Cleland (2002) Phil. Sci. 69:474; Duhem (1906)

What the walks cannot show
  • Papers report the argument, not the work. Failed attempts and reruns rarely appear. Between protocol and paper, 62% of trials changed a primary outcome (Chan et al. 2004).
  • Our coding had no field for a failed attempt, so a return and a next step look alike.
  • One coder, coding against the graph it tests: "no new node needed" is partly the instruction.
  • Granularity varies (8 to 17 steps); supplements unread for four papers; successes only.
  • Process documents (peer-review files, open discussions, versions) do record the loops: next slide.

4 · The decision tree17 / 31
Graph v0.5: three levels
  • The record: Cleland's terms H, A, C, E, and the observations O, with provenance.
  • The modes: each runs its own inner loop over graph nodes until its stopping rule holds (estimation: misfit at noise level, resolution stated; exploration: the phenomenon repeats).
  • The controller: a decision tree that reads the record and calls the next mode. A paper is written when the claim carries the evidence its mode owes.
  • Automated science loops inside one mode, with H and A fixed (Robot Scientist, Coscientist, adaptive design). None decides when to leave a mode or protect an auxiliary: the controller is what the tree adds.

4 · The decision tree23 / 31
How the tree could be wrong
  • Same route, different outcomes: if ordinary and failed papers pass the same questions in the same order, the tree describes papers, not success.
  • Missing questions: if review files or preprints show loops to a point the tree lacks (reframing the question), add it.
  • No gain for agents: if tree-following agents do no better than agents choosing modes freely on held-out cases.
  • Unobservable: if two coders agree on the question a step answers less often than κ = 0.6.
Each is a test the seminar can run
