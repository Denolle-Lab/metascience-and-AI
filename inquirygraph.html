inquirygraph: the coding agent and its gold set

Drafted v0.1 · October 25, 2026 · paper codebook 1.5.0 · loop codebook 0.1.2 · human gold set in preparation

  • What it is. A codebook, a JSON schema, and a coding agent that turn a research paper into a walk on the graph of inquiry: where the work enters, each step the authors took (a graph node and one of the thirteen modes), how it ended, and which step decided it. Every step carries a quote of at most 25 words and its page or paragraph, so each coded claim can be checked against the paper.
  • Why. To compare how people do research with how AI agents loop, on one vocabulary. The same graph codes agent systems with a sister codebook.
  • Status. Working tool of the seminar, to become a standalone package (working name inquirygraph). Results are reported here; the downloaded papers are not shared.

Try it on a paper

  • Open the evaluation page. Look up a paper by DOI or open its PDF from your computer (the file stays in your browser and is never uploaded). Then record where the work starts, which of the thirteen modes it uses, the order in which they ran, and how it answered each question of the decision tree. Each answer takes a quote and a page; with the PDF open, the page checks that the quote is on that page.
  • Your answers stay with you. The page sends nothing. It saves a draft in your browser and downloads your answers as one JSON file, which you send to the coordinators. The questions are generated from the same codebook the coding agent uses, so human and model answers can be compared field by field.

  • Vary and replicate follows NASEM (2019). Each check of a result is a reproduction, replication, method, robustness, control, or generalization check. V is full when a check other than a reproduction is done in the paper; triangulation is two or more of replication, method, and generalization (National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine 2019; Munafò and Davey Smith 2018). Papers and agent loops use the same rule.

The gold set

Step Count Rule
Pool 14,223 2022 research articles in Earth-science venues with a full-text route, in the convenor’s research topics (core ring) and neighbouring Earth-science subfields (broad ring)
Preselection 600 Equal numbers per ring and impact quintile, subfields interleaved
Eligible 525 Two independent screeners per abstract, a third on disagreements; 594 of 600 agreed
Gold set 100 Spread over impact, novelty-proxy, and reference-breadth terciles within each ring; advance is rated by people
Human subset 40 Each coded independently by two of four coders (20 papers each), blind to all metrics
  • Impact: OpenAlex field-normalized citation percentile. Novelty proxy: rarity of the paper’s two leading topics appearing together in 2017 to 2021. Breadth: share of references outside geoscience. The three are weakly correlated (Spearman 0.09 to 0.25), so they sample different things.
  • Every selection rule, threshold, and exclusion is logged with the script and commit that applied it.

What has been measured

Test Result
Two blind model coders, 13 papers (paper codebook 1.3.0) Node-set overlap 0.76; Krippendorff’s alpha for node presence 0.63, below the 0.67 gate
Two blind model coders, 31 agent systems (loop codebook 0.1.2) Alpha 0.73 for the node marks
Input format, six papers coded twice from publisher XML text and twice from PDF text Agreement between formats is within coder noise; quotes from XML text are found word for word in the source 100% of the time, against 83% from PDF text
Cost $0.27 (PDF) to $0.39 (PDF plus XML text) per paper, batch prices
  • Under way. Model coding of the 525 eligible papers (once) and the 100 gold papers (a second, independent time); human coding of the 40-paper subset. The human codings are the reference that decides whether the model coder is reliable enough to scale.
