From modes to agents: design notes

Drafted proposed for meeting 13 · not yet discussed · full text in the private repository

Three questions

  • What do the readings give us for building better scientific agents?
  • What do we do with such agents next to the deep-research tools in the frontier models?
  • Do we have to pull all the papers first?

One project to think with

  • Dense array in the Pacific Northwest; three years of ambient-noise cross-correlations
  • Seismic velocity between station pairs changes a little with the seasons
  • Question: does the seasonal change track groundwater?
  • The project passes through most of the ten modes:
    1. Observation and description: velocity-change series for every pair; nothing claimed
    2. Methods and computation: the measurement has settings that shape what can be seen
    3. Exploratory discovery: some pairs show an annual cycle, some none, one an unexpected jump
    4. Hypothesis testing: groundwater vs thermoelastic strain vs precipitation loading vs a noise-source change; different phase lags, different depths
    5. Simulation and prediction: a poroelastic model turns a well record into a predicted velocity change
    6. Synthesis and reconstruction: wells, rain, temperature, noise direction, one account
    7. Replication and validation: a second array, a second year
  • The paper will present step 4; steps 1 to 3 become one sentence (Holmes 1987)
  • That gap is standing question 5, and the first thing an agent must be honest about

Two words

  • Mode: the kind of work, by what it does for knowledge; ten in the glossary
  • Gate: a rule that stops the next step until a condition is met
    • Action gate: no step without a stated hypothesis. The seminar’s target
    • Claim gate: no claim without a test it could have failed. Kept everywhere
  • In the project: an action gate at step 3 forbids looking; a claim gate at step 4 forbids “groundwater” until rivals are excluded and the result holds on data not used to find it

What the readings give an agent designer

  • Not new: 1980s discovery programs were built from historians’ reconstructions (Langley 1981; Langley et al. 1987; Kulkarni and Simon 1988; Darden 1991; Thagard 1988); the fields converged on discovery as search in two spaces (Klahr and Simon 1999; Klahr and Dunbar 1988); review in Sozou et al. (2017)
  • New: a language model now proposes the hypotheses, and the one controlled test says it does that badly (Si, Hashimoto, and Yang, 2025, arXiv:2506.20803; Si et al. (2025))
  • Six things an agent must carry, each shown on the project:
  1. A mode vocabulary with recognition rules
    • Step 3 is exploratory discovery; step 4 is hypothesis testing; each owes different evidence
    • Agent: labels every task with a mode and reports the evidence that mode owes
  2. Transition rules between modes
    • Explore until it repeats, then test (Cleland 2002)
    • Pursue an anomaly only after it reproduces (Fugelsang et al. 2004)
    • Models tie on amplitude: find the observable they disagree on (Beven and Freer 2001; Kirchner 2006)
    • No hypothesis yet: search the experiment space (Klahr and Dunbar 1988)
    • Agent: changes mode when a condition is met, and records why
  3. Stopping and abstention
    • Open-system models cannot be confirmed (Oreskes et al. 1994); inverse problems falsify, never verify (Tarantola 2006); the decisive trace may not exist yet (Cleland 2002)
    • Project: “not decidable with three years; a fourth year or a well inside the array would decide it”
    • Agent: returns that as a result; no benchmark in the prior art rewards it
  4. Evidence labelled by kind
    • Measurement, model output, cited paper, remembered decision: different records (Holmes 1987)
    • Agent: carries the label on every claim
  5. A failure mode per mode, which becomes a test
    • Wrong controls fixed before data (Cleland 2002); post-hoc pattern reported as predicted (Ioannidis 2005; Geller 1997); fit mistaken for truth (Oreskes et al. 1994); loop scoring itself (Leeman et al. 2024)
    • Agent: each becomes a negative control in the evaluation protocol
  6. The objective, stated
    • Strongest gate: expected information gain over a model set (Rainforth et al. 2024)
    • Alternatives: novelty search (Lehman and Stanley 2011); compression progress (Schmidhuber 2010)
    • Gating works with an exact verifier (Romera-Paredes et al. 2024); “groundwater causes the change” has none
    • The verifier is the hard part (see the table below)

Coding papers by mode

  • One dichotomy is not enough: hypothesis-first vs exploration-first runs the two gates together
  • Three questions plus the mode:
    1. Hypothesis stated before the result, as presented? (action gate)
    2. A test the claim could have failed? (claim gate)
    3. Intervention or observation? (Cleland 2001)
  • Plus: who chose the question; basis of the coding

  • The system never chose the question; that is gap 1 of the prior art
  • The loop’s verifier was the weak part, not its hypothesis
    • A-Lab: 36 of 57 targets in 17 days (Szymanski et al. 2023, 86)
    • Reanalysis: most misidentified, the rest already known; a “positive testing strategy” is inadequate; a new-material claim needs rivals excluded (Leeman et al. 2024, 4)
    • Chamberlin’s family of hypotheses (Chamberlin 1965) and Cleland’s rivals, missing from the scoring step
    • A gate on the action, none on the claim
  • The thesis survives the split: in Rouet-Leduc et al. (2017) an action gate would have hurt (the finding came from “noise”); the claim gate helped (held-out data)
  • Basis has to be a column: memory-based claims in meeting 1 were wrong in the same direction

The group’s own agents

  • GAIA: thirteen role-specialized subagents under one orchestrator; read-only auditor; lab notebook; provenance keeper; human gates; design review first
  • Evaluation vault: golden items with planted flaws, decisive tests, clean controls
  • Built already: item 3 (abstention as temperament), item 4 (the notebook records the trajectory), item 5 (planted-flaw items)
  • Absent: item 1 (no mode label; the study designer’s plan format asks for hypotheses, so the project could not start at step 1), item 2 (no transition rules)
  • Item 6 decided by accident in the roster’s favour: one design review, then iterate on results (a claim gate, not an action gate)
  • Change list, in order:
    1. Mode label carried by the orchestrator
    2. Study designer that can plan observation, exploration, and instrument work without a hypothesis
    3. Auditor verdict fields: rivals enumerated; not decidable; was the claim gated
    4. Ledger rows written by the lab notebook
    5. Mode axis in the vault’s item schema
  • Meeting 13 carries this as its exercise; the roster stays private

Next to deep research

  • What the deep-research tools do: take a question, search, read, return a cited report (Huang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.18096; Du et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.11763; Skarlinski et al., 2024, arXiv:2409.13740; Shao et al. (2024); Asai et al., 2024, arXiv:2411.14199)
  • Measured caution: retrieving the benchmark’s own answer inflates scores (2026, arXiv:2606.05241)
  • In the glossary’s terms: one mode, literature-based synthesis, performed without knowing it
  • On the project, a generic report would treat a model, a field observation, and a retrospective as the same evidence; would not say who stated a hypothesis first; would not say the phase-lag question is open
  • Four additions, in build order:
    1. Mode-aware reader: per paper, the mode, the three questions, the kind of record, who chose the question; output an evidence profile, not a synthesis
    2. Trajectory reconstructor: dated record of one discovery; a ledger row with its evidence basis; flag narrative-vs-trajectory gaps
    3. Rival enumerator: before “confirmed” or “novel”, the alternatives and the observation that separates them; Platt’s touchstone (Platt 1964, 352); the A-Lab case as the test
    4. Abstention outcome: “not decidable with this evidence” plus what would decide it, scored as correct
  • Two rules across all four: record who chose the question; never cite the agent’s own reasoning as evidence
  • Where acting agents should be gated
    • Exact verifier: program search works (Romera-Paredes et al. 2024)
    • Positive-only verifier: the loop scores itself (Szymanski et al. 2023; Leeman et al. 2024)
    • Humans choose the problem and the bench validation: nanobody design (Swanson et al. 2025)
    • Expert evaluation, not the model’s confidence: chemistry tools (M. Bran et al. 2024)
    • An environment that requires hypotheses to score at all (Jansen et al. 2024)

What the reading does not yet establish

  • A general geoscience workflow. Three reasons:
    • The cases are episodes, not a sample; the corpus study has not run
    • The philosophy is about science in general, not geoscience
    • No project has been reconstructed from the inside
  • Readings on geoscientific method now in the optional extensions:
    • From within the field: Gilbert (1896); Johnson (1933); Frodeman (1995); Baker (1999); Schumm (1991); Elliott and Brook (2007)
    • Philosophy and history of geoscience: Kleinhans et al. (2005); Bokulich and Oreskes (2017); Currie (2018); Rudwick (1985)
    • Measured practice: 412 geoscientists, one seismic section, interpretations vary by training (Bond et al. 2007); judgment drifts and herds (Polson and Curtis 2010; Baddeley et al. 2004); how geoscientists think (Kastens et al. 2009)
    • The geoscience research workflow specified end to end (Gil et al. 2016, 2018)
  • Two options for the group:
    • A fourteenth meeting on how geoscientists reason: Gilbert 1896 paired with Bond et al. 2007
    • Or fold that pair into meeting 7
  • A tenth ledger row from one of the group’s own projects, reconstructed from its notebooks, would be the first evidence about trajectory rather than narrative; the lab-notebook agent produces that record

Do we have to pull the papers first?

  • For the design: no; the codebook is the glossary
  • For the sealed analyses: yes, the 31 assigned papers; memory-based analyses were wrong where it mattered
  • For the corpus study: yes, full texts of the seismology papers it codes
  • For a reading agent: yes, a retrieval corpus with page-level citations
  • About half the references have a legal open copy; tools/fetch_open_copies.py fetches those and lists the rest; paywalled ones go to the shared folder
  • Check every fetched file against its title: the index pointed at a wrong document once

Build order

  1. Mode-aware reader, validated on the corpus study’s human-coded papers
  2. Rival enumerator, as the reader’s verifier, tested on the A-Lab case
  3. Trajectory reconstructor, on the nine seminar cases, then on one of the group’s own projects
  4. Abstention, as an outcome the evaluation scores
