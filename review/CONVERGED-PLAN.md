# Converged plan: scientific advance as the outcome, plural modes as the means

September 10, 2026. This report answers one question: will Astra's `PROJECT-REVISION-PLAN.md` and my `REVISION-PLAN.md` lead to the project the convenor described today? It assesses both plans against that description, names what neither plan does, and sets out one converged plan. Nothing below has been applied.

**The convenor's description, as four criteria.**

- **A1. Scientific advance is the main outcome** that scientists, and the agents, aim for.
- **A2. Advance is reached by measuring and quantifying novelty, correctness, and impact.**
- **A3. Agents reflect the plural epistemic modes of research** as they occur in the geosciences, borrowing from other disciplines where that helps, as documented by the science of scientific process and impact, the philosophy of science, and the history of science.
- **A4. Agents are designed around human-defined epistemic modes and evaluated against past work.**

**Short answer.** Both plans lead toward A3 and most of A4's first half. Neither plan gets to A1, A2, or the second half of A4 on its own. Both treat advance as one question among several rather than the outcome variable; neither defines correctness at all, and the book's glossary has no such term; neither defines how novelty, correctness, and impact combine into a judgment of advance; and neither designs an evaluation against past work, since both evaluate on constructed task packets and treat historical replay as a teaching exercise because of training-data leakage. The converged plan below keeps what the two plans agree on and adds five things: an operational advance construct with correctness as a term of assessment; a mode specification that ties each family of epistemic work to its philosophical, historical, and bibliometric evidence; the identification of the correctness rubric with the epistemic-work layer, which is the link between A2 and A3; a retrospective evaluation against past work with a leakage protocol, using the group's own past projects as the primary set; and the corpus study rescoped from optional to the project's only source of quantification at scale.

## 2. Advance, defined so it can be measured

### 2.1 The construct

A contribution is an advance to the degree that it is new, correct, and taken up. The three components are the convenor's three measures, and each already has a partial home in the book.

| Component | Definition for this project | Where it is measured now | What is missing |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Novelty | Newness of a specific claim relative to a stated corpus and date | Novelty Dossier v1; Uzzi et al. (2013); Fontana et al. (2020); Shibayama et al. (2021) | Time indexing: novelty must be computed at the date of the claim, on a corpus cut at that date |
| Correctness | The claim is warranted by the evidence its kind of epistemic work owes, and it survives later checks | Nowhere as a term. Pieces sit in meetings 5, 6, and 9 and in the glossary's evidence column | A definition; a per-claim instrument; a proxy at scale |
| Impact | Uptake that changes what others can know, measure, explain, or do | Field-and-year-normalized citations; disruption with its corrections; canonical status; delayed recognition; data and method adoption | Separation of attention from use; a lag policy; for new work, a prediction logged now and scored later |

Correctness has two parts, and the split matters for agents. Correctness at the time is mode-specific: an estimate owes resolution and uncertainty, a regularity owes out-of-sample fit and a stated range, a discrimination owes enumerated rivals and an outcome that could have failed, a reconstruction owes convergent independent traces. That is the epistemic-work layer both plans already define, and it is the correctness rubric. Correctness over time is survival: replicated, not contradicted, still relied on after a stated interval, and revised in ways that preserve rather than overturn the claim. Bird (2007) is the philosophical anchor for putting correctness inside advance; Dellsén (2016), already in the book, is the reply that understanding can advance without new truths. Meeting 12 reads both.

### 2.2 The primary outcome and the components

The primary outcome is an ordinal advance rating given by blinded assessors: none, incremental, enabling, field-shaping. The three components are reported beside it as the evidence for it, and the assessors see the components before they rate. This keeps advance as the single outcome the convenor asked for while honouring the point both audits made: the components measure different things, and a fixed weighting would hide that. A decision rule for an agent may combine them into a utility, with the weights stated and a sensitivity analysis reported.

For past work, all three components can be measured, novelty at the date, correctness at the date and by survival, impact by uptake since. For new work, novelty and correctness at the date can be measured now; impact is a prediction logged with its date and scored when the record exists. The agent's expected-impact statements are therefore themselves claims with a registration gate.

### 2.3 Correctness at scale

Correctness cannot be coded for ten thousand papers by hand. Three proxies can, and they should be reported as proxies: survival, whether the claim is still cited as a result rather than as a superseded one after a stated interval; contradiction, whether a later paper in the corpus reports a failure to reproduce or an incompatible result; and correction, retractions and errata. On a coded subset, two human coders apply the per-mode rubric, and the proxies are validated against them.

### 2.4 The three definitions fixed in meeting 1

The glossary's "terms of assessment" gains a correctness entry with the two-part definition above, and meeting 1's "definitions to fix" list becomes: novelty, correctness, impact, then depth, interdisciplinarity, and field-shaping as secondary terms. Meeting 9 tests the correctness definition on model claims, meeting 10 the novelty definition, meeting 12 the impact definition and the whole construct.

## 3. The mode specification: what the agents are designed around

Criterion A3 asks that the modes be documented by three literatures. Neither plan produces that documentation as an artifact; the glossary has philosophy and cases, and almost no science-of-science evidence per family. The converged plan makes the specification a deliverable, one file per family of epistemic work, in `modes/` in the public repository, at bullet level, with the prose in the private repository as the book's convention requires.

Each file has six sections:

| Section | Content | Source |
|:--|:--|:--|
| What the work does for knowledge | The family's epistemic function and its recognition rule | Glossary |
| What a claim of this kind owes | The correctness rubric for the family, with the failure it guards against | Philosophy of science: Cleland, Oreskes, Tal, Bogen and Woodward, Parker, Chang, as assigned |
| Where it succeeded and where it failed | Two or more documented geoscience episodes, with the record type stated | History of science and the seminar's cases: Atwater, Wilson and Sykes, Lindsey, Parkfield, the precursor literature, and the episode record as it fills |
| What is borrowed | One case from another discipline where the family's practice is better documented, and what carries over | Karaca for high-energy physics, Nersessian for bioengineering, CASP for structural biology, Knorr Cetina for the comparison |
| What the science of science measures | The family's share of the literature, its novelty and impact profile, and the confounds | The corpus study once it runs; until then, the nearest published measurements with their population stated |
| What an agent does in this family | The procedure, its inputs and outputs, its transition conditions, its abstention condition | Astra's procedure table, extended to all eight families |

Eight files, for the eight families both plans agree on: characterize, estimate, establish a regularity, construct a concept or model, discriminate explanations, forecast or simulate, reconstruct or integrate, assess robustness. Rival generation is a named sub-procedure under discriminate. Instruments and methods appear in every file as actions and contributions, not as families.

The files are the human-defined modes of criterion A4. They are written by people from the readings and the episodes, revised at each meeting, and only then encoded as procedures. The GAIA skill that both plans propose is generated from these files, not written separately.

## 4. Agents around the modes

Both plans agree on procedures selected by a planner rather than a persona per mode. The converged plan adopts Astra's procedure table and extends it to all eight families, with three additions from the advance construct.

- **Every procedure ends with a claim card.** The card carries the claim, its family, the evidence owed and the evidence supplied (correctness at the date), its novelty relative to a bounded search with the search stated, its claim status (conjecture, provisional, warranted), and, where the agent asserts one, its expected impact with the date. The card is the unit that is scored.
- **The planner's objective is expected advance under a budget**, computed from the card's components with stated weights and reported with sensitivity, as Astra's correction allows. Hard constraints remain: a claim is not labelled warranted until the evidence owed exists; abstention with a stated deciding observation is a legal terminal state.
- **The three gates are recorded, not imposed.** For every action the log records whether a hypothesis preceded it, whether a commitment was made before access to the scoring evidence, and whether the claim's status matches the evidence supplied. The comparison in section 5 tests which gate helps for which family; the design does not assume it.

Implementation follows Astra's caution and my GAIA list in that order: step 0 is to run the group's plugin once and confirm the notebook produces a record; if it does, the procedures and claim card are added to the roster as the change list in the private assessment describes; if it does not, they are implemented in the evaluation harness first.

## 5. Evaluation against past work

This is the gap. Criterion A4 says agents are evaluated against past work; both plans evaluate on constructed packets and defer real cases. The reason is real: a famous episode is in the model's training data, and withholding the papers from retrieval does not remove the outcome from the model. The converged plan does not abandon past work because of leakage; it builds the evaluation in three sets ordered by how much leakage each admits, with a protocol that measures it.

### 5.1 Three evaluation sets

| Set | Source | Leakage | What it can score |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1. The group's own past projects | Four to six completed projects reconstructed from repositories, notebooks, and dated messages, with the actual trajectory known to the group and the unpublished detail absent from any training corpus | Lowest | Full advance triple at the date; the agent's trajectory against the real one; process match judged by the people who did the work |
| 2. Documented geoscience episodes | The nine seminar cases and others with a dated record: Cascadia 1700, transform faults, the precursor claims, Parkfield, the laboratory-earthquake finding | Highest for the famous ones; measured per episode | Process scoring: the rivals enumerated, the discriminating observation proposed, the evidence demanded, the abstention where warranted. Outcome scoring only for episodes the contamination probe marks unknown |
| 3. Post-cutoff and prospective cases | Geoscience results published after the model's cutoff; the group's ongoing projects with the agent's cards logged now | None for outcomes not yet known | The complete triple, scored when the record exists; the design of Tshitoyan et al. (2019), applied to claims rather than materials |

### 5.2 The leakage protocol

For every episode in sets 1 and 2, before any run: a contamination probe asks the model directly, and through paraphrases, for the episode's outcome; the episode is marked known, partly known, or unknown, and the mark is reported with every score. Outcome scores are claimed only on unknown episodes and on set 3. Process scores are claimed on all episodes, because proposing the right discriminating observable is scored against expert judgment of what was reasonable at the date, not against the answer. Assessors do not see the policy label. Development episodes and held-out episodes are separated, and an episode used to tune a procedure is development data thereafter.

### 5.3 What is scored

For each agent claim card on a past episode: novelty at the date against a corpus cut at that date, compared with the Novelty Dossier judgment of two human coders; correctness at the date against the family's rubric, and by survival against the later record; impact, for sets 1 and 2, against uptake since, and for set 3, logged; the advance rating by blinded assessors; and abstention, credited only where the later record shows the evidence was insufficient and penalized where an answer was available. The comparison arms and budget are Astra's: a generic planner with the same checks in unstructured form, a hypothesis-testing planner allowed inspection and calibration, a routing planner using the mode specification, and a strict action-gate ablation, on six development and six held-out packets, five runs each, before any claim of general benefit. The packets are drawn from set 1 first, then set 2, and the constructed velocity-change cases both plans describe become the development set rather than the evaluation.

### 5.4 Base rates from the corpus

The corpus study supplies what a past-work evaluation needs and cannot get elsewhere: the novelty and impact distribution of each family in the seismology literature by decade, so that an agent's claim card can be scored against what a paper of that kind at that date typically achieved. Without those base rates, "this claim is novel" and "this claim will matter" have no reference class.

## 6. Quantification at scale: the corpus study, rescoped

Both plans made the corpus study optional. Under criterion A2 it is the project's only instrument for quantifying novelty and impact by mode across a literature, and it supplies the base rates in section 5.4. It stays second in order, after the episode pilot shows that the labels can be applied with agreement, and it is no longer optional.

Its design takes every amendment both audits agreed on: a documented step-0 search; multi-label coding with the eight families and an uncodable outcome; a probability-based sample plus enriched rare-family cases reported separately; a topic screen inside broad journals with sensitivity reported; a second physical-science corpus; per-label validation stratified by family, period, and access, with calibration and prevalence correction and classifier uncertainty carried into the analysis; depth and breadth not entered as independent regressors; confounders and mediators decided before adjustment; comparable citation windows; and a causal question stated before any association is read as an effect. Two things are added: correctness proxies on a coded subset as in section 2.3, validated against human rubric coding; and the family-level novelty and impact profiles delivered as tables into the mode specification files.

## 9. What this plan does not claim

That the ordinal advance rating is valid; meeting 12 tests it on nine episodes and records disagreement. That correctness proxies at scale track correctness; the coded subset decides. That set 1 is leakage-free; the probe decides per episode. That routing by mode raises advance; the comparison decides. That breadth raises advance; that is the hypothesis, now stated with the same three measures as everything else.
