## Entry state: what the label records

Draft, October 27, 2026. Numbers from `tools/roadmap/g21_entry_state.py` (steps 4 and 9), data in `private/data/roadmap/derived/entry/entry_state.json`. Figure: `private/data/roadmap/figures/paper/fig13_entry_state.png`. Full analysis and the codebook 1.6.0 draft: `private/analysis/entry-state-evaluation.md`.

Each coded paper carries an *entry state*: a single label for how the research began, chosen from seven options defined in the codebook (version 1.5.0). The options are an anomaly (a surprising observation), a problem (an unsolved question the literature names), a challenged result (a published finding the paper sets out to check), a hypothesis stated in advance, data collected without a guiding question, a capability (a new instrument, method or archive), and an external goal (a mandate, benchmark or mission). The coder must quote the sentence that supports its choice. In round 1, the model coder labelled 78% of 233 papers as starting from a problem.

That share raised a concern familiar to readers of scientific prose. Most research articles contain a gap statement in the introduction, a sentence such as "the controls on X remain poorly understood". Swales (1990) described this as the second move of a standard rhetorical pattern: establish the field, point to a gap, then fill it. If the coder quotes that sentence, every paper looks problem-driven, whatever actually set the work going.

We tested this with a blind audit. From the round-1 codings we drew 60 papers, stratified by label (36 coded `problem`, four of each other state). Two further coders, A and B, re-coded the entry from the full text without seeing the original label or each other's work. Besides the entry state, each recorded the *operational start*, meaning the thing the study could not have begun without: data already in hand, an event, a new instrument or deployment, a new method or model, a mandate, a surprising observation, a prior result to test, a stated hypothesis, or only a gap in the literature. Each coder also flagged whether its quoted sentence was a gap statement.

Figure 13 summarizes the result. Both blind coders kept the `problem` label for 20 of the 36 `problem` papers (56%). They moved the rest mostly to capability (5 papers) and to data without theory (3). Labels other than `problem` were confirmed in 20 of 24 papers. Reweighted to the full round-1 set, the share of `problem` falls from 78% to about 44%, with a 95% interval of 31 to 57%. The quote behind a `problem` label was a gap statement in 84 to 88% of cases, and no other label was ever supported by one. Asked what started the work, the two coders agreed on "a literature gap only" for just 3 of the 36 `problem` papers and named a concrete object, usually data in hand or a new method, for 22.

Agreement between coders is reported with Krippendorff's alpha, which scales observed agreement against the agreement expected by chance from how often each label is used. A value of 1 means perfect agreement and 0 means chance level. Values above 0.8 are conventionally taken as reliable and 0.667 to 0.8 as tentative. Alpha for entry state was 0.77 between two runs of the original coder, 0.81 between the blind coders, and 0.60 to 0.67 between each blind coder and the original. The label is reliable beyond chance, but it shifts when the coder is asked to look for what started the work.

We read the entry state as coded so far as a measure of how a paper frames its own beginning, not of how the work began. Our outcome analysis found no association between entry state and citation, novelty or breadth measures. That null result therefore concerns framing. For the next codebook version we propose keeping the framing label and adding a separate operational-start field, whose quote must name the object or act and not a gap.

**Limitations.** The blind coders are Claude agents of the same model family as the original coder, so their agreement is not independent evidence. The sample has 60 papers, with only four for each rare label. The audit form asked about the operational start, which may have steered the coders away from `problem`. A human gold coding of the same 60 papers is pending.
