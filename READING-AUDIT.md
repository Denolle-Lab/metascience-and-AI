## 11. Scientific polymathy: breadth, specialization, and borrowed competence

New in v0.3, as meeting 12; moved to meeting 11 on September 6, 2026 so that it does not fall in Thanksgiving week. The group's working hypothesis is that agents make individual polymathy practical again. It is entered as a hypothesis with a stated disconfirming observation, not as a premise, and the pair deliberately predates the technology so the terms are set before it is introduced. Burke supplies the historical retrospective; note that a prosopography of recognized polymaths is selected on success and cannot establish how often breadth failed. Kitcher supplies the argument that specialization is a rational collective strategy rather than a decline, which is the strongest available objection to the hypothesis on its own terms. Trust and epistemic dependence, present-day career costs of breadth, and a measurement of epistemic diversity in language models are optional extensions rather than a fourth and fifth required paper.

## 12. Scientific advance: cumulative work and field-shaping change

Retain the Wu-Petersen methodological pairing as the required pair. Keep progress definitions, gatekeeping, and delayed recognition as optional extensions.

## 13. What architecture follows from practice, and what would show it worked?

Merged in v0.3 from the v0.2 meetings 12 and 13. No journal PDFs are bundled. The original publication or proceedings, rather than a preprint or blog summary, is the cited version wherever identified. Book chapters and first-person accounts are labeled according to their evidential role, not silently treated as controlled studies of research practice.

## Addendum, September 6, 2026: optional extensions

The thirteen required pairs are unchanged. Fifty-seven peer-reviewed papers were added as optional extensions, every meeting now has at least one, and each entry on a session page says in a sentence or two why it is there. Every DOI was verified against the Crossref registry before it was added, and the bibliographic fields were taken from the registry record rather than typed from memory.

The additions do three things. They give the hypothesis-testing loop its best published defenders (Ioannidis 2005; Geller 1997 and the Parkfield papers; King and colleagues 2009; Rainforth and colleagues 2024), so that the seminar's thesis meets its strongest opponent in the reading rather than in a caricature. They supply the record of automated discovery before 2023 (Langley 1981; Kulkarni and Simon 1988; Schmidt and Lipson 2009; Swanson 1986), which the prior-art chapter now opens with. And they bring the measured literature on breadth, specialization, and the field-level effects of AI tools into the polymathy meeting (Jones 2009; Wuchty and colleagues 2007; Teodoridis 2018; Teodoridis and colleagues 2019; Leahey and colleagues 2017; Hao and colleagues 2026; Doshi and Hauser 2024). Chamberlin's 1890 essay on multiple working hypotheses, the geologist's original of Platt's method, joins meeting 1; the three Cascadia papers that dated the 1700 earthquake from Japanese records and tree rings join meeting 3.

Five prior-art entries previously cited as preprints now have peer-reviewed versions and were updated: Co-Scientist (Nature, 2026), Gravity-Bench and LLM-SRBench (ICML 2025), MLE-bench and DiscoveryBench (ICLR 2025).

The reasoning, and the recommendations that were not applied, are in `PLAN-AUDIT.md`.
